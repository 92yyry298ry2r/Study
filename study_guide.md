## Mathematics

### 1. Number Theory ⭐
**Overview**: Study of integers and their properties. Focuses on how numbers relate to each other through division and multiplication.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Divisibility Rules**: 2 (even), 3 (sum of digits), 4 (last 2 digits), 5 (ends in 0/5), 6 (both 2 & 3), 9 (sum of digits).
- **Prime Numbers**: Numbers with only two factors (1 and itself). 2 is the only even prime.
- **GCF/LCM**: Greatest Common Factor (largest shared divisor) and Least Common Multiple (smallest shared multiple).
**Worked Examples**:
1. Find the GCF of 12 and 18. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. GCF = 6.
2. Find the LCM of 4 and 6. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16. Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18. LCM = 12.
**Common Mistakes**: Confusing GCF and LCM. Remember: GCF ≤ numbers, LCM ≥ numbers.
**Memory Aids**: "GCF is the *Greatest* Smallest; LCM is the *Least* Biggest."
**Quick Recall Box**:
- 2 is the only even prime.
- 0 and 1 are neither prime nor composite.
- Prime numbers under 20: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19.
- Product of two numbers = GCF × LCM.
- All numbers ending in 0 or 5 are divisible by 5.
**Practice Questions**:
1. Which is prime? A) 9 B) 15 C) 21 D) 23 (Ans: D)
2. GCF of 24 and 36? (Ans: 12)
3. LCM of 15 and 20? (Ans: 60)
4. Sum of first 5 primes? (Ans: 2+3+5+7+11=28)
5. Is 123 divisible by 3? (Ans: Yes, 1+2+3=6)

### 2. Factoring Polynomials ⭐
**Overview**: Breaking down complex expressions into simpler products. The reverse of multiplication.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Common Monomial Factor**: $ax + ay = a(x + y)$
- **Difference of Squares**: $a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)$
- **Sum/Difference of Cubes**: $a^3 \pm b^3 = (a \pm b)(a^2 \mp ab + b^2)$
- **Quadratic Trinomials**: $x^2 + (a+b)x + ab = (x+a)(x+b)$
**Worked Examples**:
1. Factor $x^2 - 9$: $(x+3)(x-3)$.
2. Factor $x^2 + 5x + 6$: $(x+2)(x+3)$.
**Common Mistakes**: Forgetting to factor out the GCF first. Sum of squares ($a^2+b^2$) is NOT factorable in real numbers.
**Memory Aids**: "SOAP" for cubes: **S**ame sign, **O**pposite sign, **A**lways **P**ositive.
**Quick Recall Box**:
- $a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)$
- $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
- $a^3 + b^3 = (a+b)(a^2-ab+b^2)$
- $a^3 - b^3 = (a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)$
- Check GCF first!
**Practice Questions**:
1. Factor $4x^2 - 25$. (Ans: $(2x-5)(2x+5)$)
2. Factor $x^2 - 7x + 10$. (Ans: $(x-5)(x-2)$)
3. Factor $x^3 + 8$. (Ans: $(x+2)(x^2-2x+4)$)
4. Factor $2x^2 - 8$. (Ans: $2(x-2)(x+2)$)
5. Factor $x^2 + 6x + 9$. (Ans: $(x+3)^2$)

### 3. Logarithmic and Exponential Equations ⭐
**Overview**: Converting between exponents ($b^x = y$) and logarithms ($\log_b y = x$).
**Core Concepts**:
- **Conversion**: $b^x = y \iff \log_b y = x$.
- **Product Rule**: $\log_b (MN) = \log_b M + \log_b N$.
- **Quotient Rule**: $\log_b (M/N) = \log_b M - \log_b N$.
- **Power Rule**: $\log_b (M^k) = k \cdot \log_b M$.
**Worked Examples**:
1. Convert $2^3 = 8$ to log: $\log_2 8 = 3$.
2. Solve $\log_3 x = 2$: $x = 3^2 = 9$.
**Common Mistakes**: $\log(M+N)$ is NOT $\log M + \log N$.
**Memory Aids**: "The base stays the base."
**Quick Recall Box**:
- $\log_b b = 1$
- $\log_b 1 = 0$
- $\log_b (b^x) = x$
- $\ln x$ is $\log_e x$
- $\log x$ is $\log_{10} x$
**Practice Questions**:
1. Value of $\log_5 25$? (Ans: 2)
2. Solve $10^x = 1000$. (Ans: 3)
3. Expand $\log(xy^2)$. (Ans: $\log x + 2\log y$)
4. Value of $\ln e^4$? (Ans: 4)
5. Solve $\log_2 x = 5$. (Ans: 32)

### 4. Trigonometry and Identities ⭐
**Overview**: Study of triangle side relationships and angles, mapped onto the Unit Circle.
**Core Concepts**:
- **SOH CAH TOA**: $\sin = O/H, \cos = A/H, \tan = O/A$.
- **Pythagorean Identity**: $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$.
- **Reciprocals**: $\csc = 1/\sin, \sec = 1/\cos, \cot = 1/\tan$.
- **Unit Circle**: Radius = 1. $(x, y) = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.
**Worked Examples**:
1. If $\sin\theta = 3/5$, find $\cos\theta$: $\sqrt{1 - (3/5)^2} = 4/5$.
2. Find $\tan(45^\circ)$: $\sin(45)/\cos(45) = 1$.
**Common Mistakes**: Confusing radians and degrees. $\pi \text{ radians} = 180^\circ$.
**Memory Aids**: "All Students Take Calculus" (ASTC) for sign of functions in quadrants I-IV.
**Quick Recall Box**:
- $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$
- $\tan = \sin/\cos$
- $30^\circ = \pi/6, 45^\circ = \pi/4, 60^\circ = \pi/3, 90^\circ = \pi/2$
- $\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$
- $\cos 60^\circ = 0.5$
**Practice Questions**:
1. $\sin^2(10^\circ) + \cos^2(10^\circ) = ?$ (Ans: 1)
2. $\tan\theta = ?$ in terms of $\sin/\cos$. (Ans: $\sin\theta/\cos\theta$)
3. $\sin(90^\circ) = ?$ (Ans: 1)
4. Quadrant where $\sin$ and $\cos$ are both negative? (Ans: III)
5. Reciprocal of $\sec\theta$? (Ans: $\cos\theta$)

### 5. Geometry ⭐
**Overview**: Properties of shapes, lines, and solids. Focus on area, volume, and angles.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Circles**: Area = $\pi r^2$, Circumference = $2\pi r$.
- **Quadrilaterals**: Sum of angles = $360^\circ$.
- **Sphere**: Volume = $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, Surface Area = $4\pi r^2$.
- **Shaded Region**: Area of Outer Shape - Area of Inner Shape.
**Worked Examples**:
1. Area of circle with $r=3$: $9\pi$.
2. Volume of sphere with $r=3$: $\frac{4}{3}\pi(27) = 36\pi$.
**Common Mistakes**: Using diameter instead of radius. Confusing volume and surface area formulas.
**Memory Aids**: Area is "square" units ($r^2$), Volume is "cubic" units ($r^3$).
**Quick Recall Box**:
- Sum of triangle angles = $180^\circ$
- Pythagorean Theorem: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$
- Area of triangle = $0.5 \cdot b \cdot h$
- Volume of cylinder = $\pi r^2 h$
- Diagonal of square = $s\sqrt{2}$
**Practice Questions**:
1. Area of square with $s=4$? (Ans: 16)
2. Circumference of circle with $d=10$? (Ans: $10\pi$)
3. Volume of cube with $s=2$? (Ans: 8)
4. Third angle of triangle if two are $50^\circ, 60^\circ$? (Ans: $70^\circ$)
5. Area of circle if $C=6\pi$? (Ans: $9\pi$)

## Statistics

### 1. Central Tendency ⭐
**Overview**: Measures that represent the "center" of a data set.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Mean**: Average (Sum / count). Sensitive to outliers.
- **Median**: Middle value when sorted. Robust to outliers.
- **Mode**: Most frequent value. Can have multiple or none.
**Worked Examples**:
1. Find mean of 2, 4, 6: $(2+4+6)/3 = 4$.
2. Find median of 1, 3, 10: 3.
**Common Mistakes**: Not sorting data before finding the median.
**Memory Aids**: "Mean is average, Median is middle, Mode is most."
**Quick Recall Box**:
- Mean = $\sum x / n$
- Median is 50th percentile.
- Outliers affect Mean most.
- Symmetric data: Mean ≈ Median ≈ Mode.
- Skewed data: Mean is pulled toward the tail.
**Practice Questions**:
1. Mean of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5? (Ans: 3)
2. Median of 10, 2, 5? (Ans: 5)
3. Mode of 2, 2, 3, 4? (Ans: 2)
4. Which is best for salaries with one billionaire? (Ans: Median)
5. Mean of first 3 even numbers? (Ans: 4)

### 2. Hypothesis Testing ⭐
**Overview**: Using statistics to decide if a claim about a population is likely true.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Null ($H_0$)**: Status quo, no effect, "equal to."
- **Alternative ($H_a$)**: What you want to prove, "not equal," "greater than," "less than."
- **P-value**: Probability of getting results if $H_0$ is true. If $p < \alpha$, reject $H_0$.
- **Type I Error ($\alpha$)**: Rejecting $H_0$ when it's actually true (False Positive).
- **Type II Error ($\beta$)**: Failing to reject $H_0$ when it's actually false (False Negative).
**Worked Examples**:
1. If $p=0.03$ and $\alpha=0.05$, decision? Reject $H_0$.
2. $H_0: \mu = 50$. $H_a: \mu \neq 50$.
**Common Mistakes**: Thinking $p < 0.05$ means $H_a$ is 100% true.
**Memory Aids**: "If the P is low, the Null must go!"
**Quick Recall Box**:
- $\alpha$ = Sig level (usually 0.05).
- $H_0$ always has an equal sign.
- Type I = False Alarm.
- Type II = Missed Opportunity.
- Z-score = $(x - \mu) / \sigma$.
**Practice Questions**:
1. $H_0$ symbol? (Ans: $H_0$)
2. Reject $H_0$ if $p$ is? (Ans: Less than $\alpha$)
3. Type I error is also? (Ans: $\alpha$)
4. If $z=2$, how many std devs from mean? (Ans: 2)
5. Can $H_0$ be "$\mu > 10$"? (Ans: No, must be "=")

## Science

### 1. Photosynthesis ⭐
**Overview**: Process by which plants convert light energy into chemical energy (glucose).
**Core Concepts**:
- **Light Reactions**: Occur in **Thylakoids**. Uses $H_2O$ and light to produce $ATP$, $NADPH$, and $O_2$ (waste).
- **Calvin Cycle**: Occurs in **Stroma**. Uses $CO_2$, $ATP$, and $NADPH$ to produce Glucose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$).
- **Equation**: $6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light} \to C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2$.
**Worked Examples**:
1. Where is oxygen produced? Thylakoid.
2. What is the source of carbon for glucose? $CO_2$.
**Common Mistakes**: Thinking plants only do photosynthesis (they also do respiration!).
**Memory Aids**: "Stroma = Space (Calvin Cycle happens in space between thylakoids)."
**Quick Recall Box**:
- Light Rxn = Thylakoid.
- Dark Rxn (Calvin) = Stroma.
- Chlorophyll absorbs Red/Blue, reflects Green.
- $H_2O$ is split in light reaction.
- Glucose is the main product.
**Practice Questions**:
1. Waste product of photosynthesis? (Ans: $O_2$)
2. Where does Calvin Cycle occur? (Ans: Stroma)
3. Main pigment? (Ans: Chlorophyll)
4. Energy source? (Ans: Sun/Light)
5. Which gas is consumed? (Ans: $CO_2$)

### 2. Molecular Biology ⭐
**Overview**: The study of DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Base Pairing**: DNA: A-T, C-G. RNA: A-U, C-G.
- **Chargaff's Rule**: $\%A = \%T$ and $\%C = \%G$.
- **Central Dogma**: DNA $\to$ RNA $\to$ Protein.
- **Vacuole**: Cell organelle for storage (water, nutrients, waste). Large in plants.
**Worked Examples**:
1. If a DNA sample has 20% A, how much G? T is 20%. A+T = 40%. C+G = 60%. G = 30%.
2. Complement of AGT? TCA.
**Common Mistakes**: Pairing A with U in DNA questions.
**Memory Aids**: "Apples in the Tree (A-T), Cars in the Garage (C-G)."
**Quick Recall Box**:
- DNA is double-stranded.
- Nucleotides: Sugar, Phosphate, Base.
- Transcription: DNA to RNA.
- Translation: RNA to Protein.
- Vacuole = Storage tank.
**Practice Questions**:
1. DNA Base for A? (Ans: T)
2. RNA Base for A? (Ans: U)
3. Organelle for storage? (Ans: Vacuole)
4. % of G if C is 15%? (Ans: 15%)
5. Sugar in DNA? (Ans: Deoxyribose)

### 3. Stoichiometry ⭐
**Overview**: Calculating quantities of reactants and products in chemical reactions.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Mole**: $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ particles.
- **Mole Ratio**: From balanced equation coefficients.
- **Limiting Reactant**: Reactant that runs out first.
**Worked Examples**:
1. In $2H_2 + O_2 \to 2H_2O$, how many moles of $H_2O$ from 4 moles of $H_2$? 4 moles (1:1 ratio).
2. Molar mass of $H_2O$? $1(2) + 16 = 18$ g/mol.
**Common Mistakes**: Not balancing the equation first.
**Memory Aids**: "Coefficients are like recipe ratios."
**Quick Recall Box**:
- Balanced equation is mandatory.
- 1 mole gas at STP = 22.4 L.
- Mass to Moles: Divide by Molar Mass.
- Moles to Mass: Multiply by Molar Mass.
- Conservation of Mass: Reactants mass = Products mass.
**Practice Questions**:
1. Moles in 36g of $H_2O$? (Ans: 2)
2. Ratio of $H_2$ to $O_2$ in $2H_2 + O_2 \to 2H_2O$? (Ans: 2:1)
3. Avogadro's number? (Ans: $6.022 \times 10^{23}$)
4. Mass of 1 mole $O_2$? (Ans: 32g)
5. Limiting reactant runs out...? (Ans: First)

### 4. Kinematics ⭐
**Overview**: Describing the motion of objects using words, diagrams, numbers, graphs, and equations.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Velocity**: $\Delta \text{displacement} / \text{time}$.
- **Acceleration**: $\Delta \text{velocity} / \text{time}$.
- **Equations**: $v = u + at$; $s = ut + 0.5at^2$; $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$.
- **Force**: $F = ma$ (Newton's 2nd Law).
**Worked Examples**:
1. Car starts from rest, $a=2 m/s^2$. Speed after 5s? $v = 0 + 2(5) = 10 m/s$.
2. Force to move 5kg at $3 m/s^2$? $F = 5 \cdot 3 = 15 N$.
**Common Mistakes**: Forgetting that "rest" means $u=0$. Mixing units (e.g., km/h and m/s).
**Memory Aids**: "V-U-A-T" (View At) for the first equation.
**Quick Recall Box**:
- $g = 9.8 m/s^2$.
- Displacement is vector, Distance is scalar.
- Velocity is vector, Speed is scalar.
- Slope of d-t graph = Velocity.
- Slope of v-t graph = Acceleration.
**Practice Questions**:
1. Unit of Force? (Ans: Newton)
2. $a$ if $v$ goes from 0 to 20 in 4s? (Ans: $5 m/s^2$)
3. $F$ on 10kg with $a=2$? (Ans: $20 N$)
4. "At rest" means $v = ?$ (Ans: 0)
5. $g$ on Earth? (Ans: $9.8 m/s^2$)

### 5. Plate Tectonics ⭐
**Overview**: Theory that Earth's outer shell is divided into plates that glide over the mantle.
**Core Concepts**:
- **Boundaries**: Divergent (apart), Convergent (together), Transform (slide).
- **Faults**: Normal (tension), Reverse (compression), Strike-slip (shear).
- **Volcanoes**: Cinder Cone (small, steep), Shield (broad, gentle), Stratovolcano (large, explosive).
**Worked Examples**:
1. What boundary forms mid-ocean ridges? Divergent.
2. What fault is San Andreas? Strike-slip / Transform.
**Common Mistakes**: Thinking all volcanoes are the same shape.
**Memory Aids**: "Divergent = Divide; Convergent = Connect."
**Quick Recall Box**:
- Epicenter is above Focus.
- P-waves are fastest.
- S-waves cannot travel through liquid.
- Ring of Fire: Pacific Ocean.
- Densest planet: Earth.
**Practice Questions**:
1. Plate boundary moving apart? (Ans: Divergent)
2. San Andreas fault type? (Ans: Strike-slip)
3. Densest planet? (Ans: Earth)
4. Most eccentric orbit? (Ans: Mercury)
5. Scale for mineral hardness? (Ans: Moh's)

## Likely Exam Traps
1. **Units**: Checking if the answer is in meters but question gave kilometers.
2. **Not/Except**: Missing the word "NOT" in the question.
3. **Median Sorting**: Forgetting to sort numbers before finding the middle.
4. **Chargaff's Rule**: Calculating % for RNA but using T instead of U.
5. **Radicals**: Forgetting that $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$.
