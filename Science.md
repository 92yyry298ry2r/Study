# Science Study Notes

## Photosynthesis
1. Occurs in chloroplasts of plant cells.
2. Light-dependent reactions happen in thylakoid membranes.
3. Light-independent reactions (Calvin Cycle) occur in stroma.
4. Thylakoids contain chlorophyll to capture light energy.
5. Stroma is fluid-filled space surrounding thylakoids.
6. Photosystem II (PSII) absorbs light to split water (photolysis).
7. Photosystem I (PSI) absorbs light to reduce NADP+ to NADPH.
8. Electron Transport Chain (ETC) links PSII and PSI.
9. ATP Synthase uses proton gradient to produce ATP.
10. Final products are Glucose (C6H12O6) and Oxygen (O2).

## Parts of a Cell
1. Nucleus contains genetic material (DNA).
2. Mitochondria produce energy (ATP) via respiration.
3. Ribosomes are sites of protein synthesis.
4. Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) transports materials (Rough has ribosomes).
5. Golgi Apparatus packages and ships proteins.
6. Lysosomes contain enzymes for digestion/waste breakdown.
7. Vacuole serves as storage for metabolites, wastes, and substances.
8. Cell Membrane regulates entry/exit of substances.
9. Chloroplasts (plants only) perform photosynthesis.
10. Cytoplasm is jelly-like fluid holding organelles.

## Molecular Biology Concepts
1. DNA is double helix made of nucleotides.
2. Nucleotide consists of sugar, phosphate, and nitrogenous base.
3. Base pairing: Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T).
4. Base pairing: Cytosine (C) pairs with Guanine (G).
5. RNA uses Uracil (U) instead of Thymine (T).
6. Chargaff's Rule: Amount of A = T and G = C.
7. Total purines (A+G) = Total pyrimidines (T+C).
8. Transcription: DNA copied to mRNA in nucleus.
9. Translation: mRNA decoded to protein at ribosome.
10. Central Dogma: DNA -> RNA -> Protein.

## Stoichiometry
1. Based on Law of Conservation of Mass.
2. Balanced equations show mole ratios of reactants/products.
3. Mole (mol) is SI unit for amount of substance.
4. Avogadro's Number is 6.022 x 10^23 particles/mol.
5. Molar mass (g/mol) used for mass-to-mole conversions.
6. Theoretical yield is max product formed from limiting reactant.
7. Limiting reactant is completely consumed first.
8. Excess reactant remains after reaction stops.
9. Percent yield = (Actual / Theoretical) x 100.
10. Stoichiometric coefficients represent molar proportions.

## Isotopes
1. Atoms of same element with different neutron counts.
2. Same atomic number (protons), different mass number.
3. Chemically identical due to same electron configuration.
4. Physical properties (density, boiling point) may differ.
5. Radioactive isotopes (radioisotopes) are unstable.
6. Carbon-14 used for radiocarbon dating.
7. Hydrogen isotopes: Protium (0n), Deuterium (1n), Tritium (2n).
8. Mass spectrometer separates isotopes by mass/charge ratio.
9. Atomic mass on periodic table is weighted average.
10. Used in medicine for imaging and cancer treatment.

## Thermochemistry
1. Study of heat energy changes in chemical reactions.
2. Enthalpy (H) is total heat content of system.
3. Exothermic: System releases heat (Delta H < 0).
4. Endothermic: System absorbs heat (Delta H > 0).
5. Specific heat capacity (c) is heat to raise 1g by 1°C.
6. Calorimetry measures heat transfer in reactions.
7. Hess's Law: Total enthalpy change is path-independent.
8. Entropy (S) measures disorder/randomness.
9. Gibbs Free Energy (G) determines reaction spontaneity.
10. Q = mc(Delta T) calculates heat change.

## Physical and Chemical Changes
1. Physical change alters form/state, not composition.
2. Examples: Melting ice, boiling water, tearing paper.
3. Chemical change creates new substances via bonding.
4. Examples: Rusting iron, burning wood, souring milk.
5. Signs of chemical change: Gas production, color change.
6. Signs of chemical change: Precipitate forms, temperature change.
7. Physical changes are usually reversible (e.g., freezing).
8. Chemical changes are often irreversible without more reactions.
9. Density, solubility, and melting point are physical properties.
10. Flammability and reactivity are chemical properties.

## Kinematics
1. Describes motion without considering its causes (forces).
2. Displacement is change in position (vector).
3. Velocity is rate of change of displacement.
4. Acceleration is rate of change of velocity.
5. Formula: v = u + at (final velocity).
6. Formula: s = ut + 0.5at^2 (displacement).
7. Formula: v^2 = u^2 + 2as.
8. Gravity (g) on Earth is approx 9.8 m/s^2.
9. Scalar quantities have magnitude only; vectors have direction.

## Optics
1. Study of light behavior and properties.
2. Reflection: Light bounces off surfaces (Angle i = Angle r).
3. Refraction: Light bends when entering different media.
4. Converging lens (Convex): Bends light inward to a focal point.
5. Diverging lens (Concave): Bends light outward, appears from focal point.
6. Real image: Formed where light rays actually meet.
7. Virtual image: Rays appear to diverge from a point.
8. Lens equation: 1/f = 1/do + 1/di.
9. Magnification (m) = -di / do.
10. Total Internal Reflection Occurs when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle.

## Plate Tectonics
1. Earth's lithosphere divided into moving plates.
2. Convection currents in mantle drive plate motion.
3. Divergent boundaries: Plates move apart (mid-ocean ridges).
4. Convergent boundaries: Plates collide (mountains, subduction).
5. Transform boundaries: Plates slide past (San Andreas fault).
6. Faults are fractures in Earth's crust where movement occurs.
7. Normal fault: Hanging wall moves down (tension).
8. Reverse fault: Hanging wall moves up (compression).
9. Volcanism occurs mainly at plate boundaries and hotspots.
10. Subduction zones create volcanic arcs and trenches.

## Types of Volcanoes
1. Shield: Broad, flat, basaltic lava (e.g., Mauna Loa).
2. Cinder Cone: Small, steep, pyroclastic fragments.
3. Composite (Stratovolcano): Tall, explosive, layers (e.g., Mt. Fuji).
4. Caldera: Large collapse crater after massive eruption.
5. Fissure: Linear vent where lava erupts without explosive activity.
6. Lava Dome: Bulbous mass of viscous lava.
7. Active: Erupting or likely to erupt soon.
8. Dormant: "Sleeping," has not erupted in long time.
9. Extinct: Unlikely to erupt again.
10. Magma is molten rock underground; Lava is above ground.

## Rocks and Minerals
1. Minerals are naturally occurring, inorganic solids.
2. Mohs Scale measures mineral hardness (Talc 1 to Diamond 10).
3. Luster: How light reflects off mineral surface.
4. Streak: Color of mineral powder on porcelain plate.
5. Cleavage: Tendency to break along flat planes.
6. Igneous rocks form from cooling magma/lava.
7. Sedimentary rocks form from lithification of sediments.
8. Metamorphic rocks form under heat and pressure.
9. Rock Cycle: Constant transformation between rock types.
10. Crystalline structure determines mineral properties.

## Planets of the Solar System
1. Mercury: Smallest, closest to Sun, no traditional atmosphere.
2. Venus: Hottest due to greenhouse effect, rotates backwards.
3. Earth: Only planet known to support life.
4. Mars: "Red Planet," home to Olympus Mons volcano.
5. Jupiter: Largest planet, Great Red Spot storm.
6. Saturn: Extensive ring system made of ice/dust.
7. Uranus: Rotates on its side (98-degree tilt).
8. Neptune: Windiest planet, Great Dark Spot.
9. Earth is densest planet (5.51 g/cm^3).
10. Mercury has most eccentric orbit (most elliptical).
